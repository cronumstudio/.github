# Cronum Studio trademark policy

Cronum Studio is the trade name under which Martin Andersen makes Cronum Work. This policy says
how you may use its names and logos.

The code of Cronum Work, its modules and suite-core is free software under the GNU Affero General
Public License v3.0 (AGPL-3.0-only). That licence covers the code, not the names and logos. As
section 7 of the AGPL allows, it grants no trademark rights in them (section 7(e)), and a modified
version that you make available to others must be marked as different from ours (section 7(c)).
The `NOTICE.md` of each code repository states these two terms. Nothing in this policy limits what
the AGPL lets you do with the code itself: you can always run, study, change and share it. When you
share a changed version, you give it your own name and look.

## Our marks

  products.
- The Cronum logos, and the app icons of Cronum Work and its modules.
- The ring, an open circle with a dot in its gap, and the "by Cronum Studio" signature it goes

The modules' own names, Tasks, Notes, Projects and Next, are ordinary words. We don't claim them on

for your own product.

## Brand files in the repositories

The logo and icon files are in the repositories so the apps can run. They are brand files, and so
are the screenshots in Cronum Work's documentation and the logos in the `profile/` folder of this
repository. Brand files are not under the AGPL. In each code repository, `NOTICE.md` lists them
and `LICENSES/LicenseRef-Cronum-Brand.txt` gives their licence; the logos in `profile/` have no
licence beyond what this policy allows. In short, keep them in unmodified copies, and replace them
in a modified version that you make available to others.

## What you can do without asking

- **Run unmodified copies, including self-hosting**, for yourself or your organization, with the
  original names, icons and signature. Your organization is you, your household, or the company,
  public body, school or association you act for, with its staff and members. Setting the
  configuration options the software offers, and installing our updates, doesn't count as
  modifying. Installing and running a copy for a client, for that client's own organization,
  counts as the client's use.
- **Change the code for your own use** or your organization's, without renaming it, as long as you
  don't make the changed version available to others.
- **Fork to contribute**: a fork or branch on GitHub or another code host, kept only to study the
  code or to work on changes, for example to propose them to us in a pull request, may keep our
  names, logos, icons and signature, as long as you don't offer it to others as a product or a
  service.
- **Share unmodified copies**, such as a download, a mirror, or a package or container image made
  without changes, with the original names and icons, as long as you don't suggest that you are
  Cronum Studio or that your copy is an official one.
- **Say truthfully what your work is based on or works with**: "based on Cronum Work", "a fork of
  Cronum Work", "works with Cronum Work". Write it in plain text, less prominent than your own
  name.
- **Refer to Cronum Studio and its products by name** in articles, reviews, tutorials, talks,
  courses and comparisons, and show our logo unchanged next to such a mention, as long as it's
  clear that you aren't Cronum Studio and that we don't endorse you.
- **Keep the notices**: the copyright and licence notices, as the AGPL asks. An unmodified copy
  may also keep the "by Cronum Studio" signature.

## What needs your own name

If you make a modified version available to others (a fork that you publish as your own project,
a rebranded build, a package, a container image, an app, or a service that runs modified code),
you must:

- give it a name that doesn't contain "Cronum" and can't be confused with our names, and use that
  name for its domain, its app-store listings and its accounts too;
- replace the Cronum logos, app icons, ring and "by Cronum Studio" signature with your own, and
  leave out our mascot;
- not suggest that Cronum Studio made, endorses or supports it.

You are welcome to say that it is based on Cronum Work ("Acme Planner, based on Cronum Work by
Cronum Studio"). Keep the copyright and licence notices, and say that you changed it, as the AGPL
asks.

## Running a service for others

If you run a copy, modified or not, as a service for people outside your organization (for
example, accounts for the public or for your customers), it needs its own name, domain and look,
as above: replace our names, logos, icons, ring and signature, and say clearly on its sign-in page
and in its terms who runs it and where its users can get help. You may say that it runs Cronum
Work. Those changes make it a modified version, so the AGPL asks you to offer its users its source
code.

## Examples

Fine:

- "Acme Planner, based on Cronum Work"
- "A fork of Cronum Work with a calendar module"
- "We self-host Cronum Work for our team"
- "How to install Cronum Work on a home server", as the title of a tutorial

Not fine without permission:

- "Cronum Work Pro", "Cronum for Teams", "Acme Cronum", "Cronum Tasks"
- a domain such as cronum-hosting.example, or an app-store listing called "Cronum Work" that
  isn't ours
- a modified build, or a service for the public, that still shows the Cronum logos, icons or
  signature

## Never without permission

  that contains "Cronum Work" or "Cronum Studio", or that people could take for one of our names,
  for software or services like ours.
- Registering our logos or icons as a trademark or design, or using them as the logo of your own
  product, project or business.
- Using our names or logos in a way that is misleading or unlawful, or that disparages Cronum
  Studio.
